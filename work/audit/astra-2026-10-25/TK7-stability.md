# TK7 stability method review

DOCUMENTED: sim/stability.sh as-built E uses DC feedback from connector through68k, local1nF feedback from op-amp output,330+470ohm isolation,100k receiver,12V ideal rail,0.1V input step. The old A-D comparisons are not the current circuit and need not be rerun for this task. `stability_current.py` executes E at1p,1n,10n,47n,100n using the source's measurement code and records its hash. Simulation results pending at this checkpoint.

INFERRED: the isolation resistance limits direct capacitive loading of the output; local feedback takes over above the68k/1nF frequency scale(~2.34kHz). A bounded, decaying step response across the requested capacitances supports nominal closed-loop stability. It does not prove phase margin, all-parts stability, or response after saturation.

Limits of the test: one positive step around1.7-1.85V output,100k resistive load,ideal12V source,no clamp conduction,one macromodel and temperature. Neither negative steps nor the4.7k receiver/supply corners are covered. The TI model is typical; output crossover, current-limit recovery, saturation recovery, supply-startup behavior and process spread may differ from hardware. A loop-gain injection test (maintaining the DC operating point and both feedback paths) is appropriate for phase margin. Bench step/oscillation tests at several output levels and real cable loads would complement it. No phase-margin value is invented from overshoot.
